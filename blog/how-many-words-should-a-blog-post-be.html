How Many Words Should a Blog Post Be? Word Count and SEO

Almost every new blogger asks the same question: how long should a post be? You will see confident answers online, from 300 words to 3,000 and beyond. The honest truth is that there is no magic number. What matters is whether your post fully answers the reader's question. This guide explains how word count and SEO really relate, how to pick a sensible length, and how to measure it.

What does Google say about word count?

Google has stated that it does not use a minimum word count as a ranking factor. A short page can rank well if it answers the question clearly, and a very long page can rank badly if it is padded with filler. Length is not the goal. Usefulness is. Longer articles often rank well simply because they cover more ground, answer related questions, and attract more links, not because the number of words itself is rewarded.

Why longer posts often perform well

  • They cover a topic thoroughly. A guide that answers five related questions satisfies more searchers than one that answers a single question.
  • They earn more links and shares. Complete resources are more likely to be referenced by other writers.
  • They match more search phrases. A detailed post naturally includes many related terms.

But long is not automatically better. If a reader only needs a quick fact, a 2,000-word article makes them dig for it, and they may leave.

Typical lengths by content type

These ranges are rough guides, not rules.

  • Quick answers and definitions: 300 to 600 words.
  • Standard blog posts and how-tos: 800 to 1,500 words.
  • In-depth guides and pillar pages: 1,500 to 3,000 words or more.
  • Product or tool pages: 300 to 800 words, with the tool or product itself as the main feature.
  • News updates: Often 300 to 500 words.

How to choose the right length for your post

  1. Search your target keyword. Look at the pages that already rank. Are they short answers or long guides? This shows what searchers expect.
  2. List the questions readers will have. Your post should answer all the important ones.
  3. Find the gaps. Note what the top pages miss, such as examples, steps, a checklist or a tool. Adding those is more valuable than adding words.
  4. Write until the topic is covered, then stop. Cut anything that repeats itself.

Reading time and your audience

Most adults read around 200 to 250 words per minute, so an 1,100-word post takes about five minutes. Think about your readers. People on phones scanning for an answer need short paragraphs, clear subheadings and a summary near the top. Students or professionals researching a subject will happily read longer, as long as the structure is easy to follow.

How to make long posts easy to read

  • Use clear subheadings every few paragraphs.
  • Keep paragraphs to two to four sentences.
  • Use lists and short examples to break up text.
  • Put a short summary or answer near the top.
  • Link to related posts so readers can go deeper, as we do in our on-page SEO checklist.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Padding. Adding fluff to reach a target hurts readability and trust.
  • Copying competitors. Matching their length without adding value gives readers no reason to choose you.
  • Publishing thin pages. Pages with only a few generic sentences rarely help anyone.
  • Ignoring updates. A long post with outdated facts is worse than a short, accurate one.

Conclusion

There is no perfect blog post length. The best word count is the one that fully and clearly answers your reader's question, no more and no less. Study what already ranks, fill the gaps those pages leave, and keep your writing easy to scan. Then check your length with a counter and publish with confidence.
