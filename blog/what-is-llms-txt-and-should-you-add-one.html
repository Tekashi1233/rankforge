What Is an llms.txt File and Should You Add One in 2026?

Quick answer: llms.txt is a simple text file you place at the root of your site. It gives AI systems clear instructions about how they may use and cite your content. It is optional, low-effort, and increasingly useful for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

In the last two years a new file has started appearing on websites: llms.txt. It sits in the same place as robots.txt and serves a similar purpose — except it is written for large language models and AI answer engines rather than traditional search crawlers. This guide explains what it is, how it differs from robots.txt, and whether you should bother creating one in 2026.

What is llms.txt?

llms.txt is a plain-text file placed at the root of a website (for example https://yoursite.com/llms.txt). It contains human-readable instructions that tell AI systems:

  • Whether they may use the site’s content to answer questions
  • How the site prefers to be cited or attributed
  • Which pages or sections are most important
  • Any extra notes the site owner wants AI systems to see

Unlike robots.txt, which uses a strict technical format, llms.txt is written in normal language so that language models can understand it easily.

How is it different from robots.txt?

Featurerobots.txtllms.txt
Main audienceSearch engine crawlersLarge language models & answer engines
FormatStrict technical rulesPlain, readable text
PurposeControl crawling and indexingGuide usage, citation and understanding
EnforcementWidely respected by major crawlersStill emerging — not universal

You can (and usually should) have both files. robots.txt still controls which bots may crawl your pages. llms.txt gives higher-level guidance to the AI systems that read those pages.

Does llms.txt actually help with GEO?

Generative Engine Optimization is still young, so hard data is limited. In practice, sites that already have clear structure, good answers, and solid technical foundations tend to perform better in AI answers. An llms.txt file is a small extra signal that can:

  • Reduce the chance of incorrect or incomplete citations
  • Point AI systems toward your best or most authoritative pages
  • State your preferred attribution clearly
  • Signal that you are actively thinking about AI usage

It is not a ranking factor in the traditional sense, and it will not magically make AI systems cite you. It is a low-cost way to make your preferences explicit.

Should you add one in 2026?

Yes, if:

  • You want AI systems to cite your content accurately
  • You have a clear preferred way to be attributed
  • You already care about GEO and AI visibility
  • You can create the file in a few minutes

You can skip it (for now) if:

  • Your site is brand new and still thin
  • You have more important technical or content issues to fix first
  • You are not yet focused on AI answer visibility

For most established sites the cost is near zero and the upside is clearer communication with AI systems. That makes it an easy win.

Best practices

  • Keep the file short and clear.
  • Use plain language — AI systems read it as text.
  • Update it when your preferred citation or policy changes.
  • Combine it with a clean robots.txt (especially rules for AI crawlers) and well-structured content.
  • Test that the file is publicly accessible after uploading.

Frequently asked questions

Is llms.txt required in 2026?

No. It is optional. Many sites appear in AI answers without one. It simply gives clearer guidance to AI systems that choose to read it.

Does every AI system respect llms.txt?

Not yet. Support is growing but not universal. The file is still worth adding because the cost is extremely low.

Where do I put the llms.txt file?

In the root of your domain, exactly like robots.txt: https://yoursite.com/llms.txt

Can I block AI training with this file?

You can state your preference. Whether a particular model respects it depends on that model’s policy. For stronger technical control, also review the AI-crawler rules in your robots.txt.

Conclusion

llms.txt is a simple, low-effort file that gives AI systems clearer instructions about how to use and cite your content.
