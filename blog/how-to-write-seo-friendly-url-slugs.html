How to Write SEO-Friendly URL Slugs

Every page on your website has an address, and the last part of that address is called the slug. It looks like a small detail, but a clear slug helps visitors understand where a link leads and helps search engines see what a page is about. This guide explains what slugs are, how to write good ones, and how to avoid problems when you need to change them.

What is a URL slug?

A slug is the part of a web address that identifies one specific page. In the address yourdomain.com/blog/how-to-write-url-slugs, the slug is how-to-write-url-slugs. It usually comes from the page title but is shortened and cleaned so that it is easy to read and safe to use in links.

Why slugs matter

  • They help people. A readable slug tells a visitor what to expect before they click, which builds trust.
  • They help sharing. Short, clear links look better in messages, emails and social posts.
  • They give search engines context. The words in a URL are a small signal about the topic. They will not rescue weak content, but they support good content.
  • They make your site easier to manage. Consistent slugs keep your pages organized.

Best practices for SEO-friendly slugs

  1. Keep it short. Three to five meaningful words is usually enough.
  2. Use hyphens between words. Search engines read hyphens as spaces. Underscores can join words into one long term.
  3. Write in lowercase. Some servers treat upper and lower case as different addresses, which can create duplicates.
  4. Include your main keyword. Use the phrase people would search for, naturally.
  5. Remove filler words. Words like "the", "and", "of" and "a" can often be dropped without losing meaning.
  6. Avoid special characters. Symbols, spaces and accents can break links or turn into confusing codes. Letters, numbers and hyphens are safest.
  7. Avoid dates unless needed. A year in the slug can make content look old and forces you to change the address when you update the page.
  8. Describe the page, not the category. Make it specific enough that it could only belong to that page.

Good and bad examples

Bad: yourdomain.com/page?id=4821

This tells nobody anything and is hard to remember.

Should slugs match the page title exactly?

Not necessarily. The title can be long and written to attract clicks, while the slug stays short and plain. For example, a title such as "How Long Should a Meta Description Be? (With Examples)" can have the slug meta-description-length. The slug only needs to describe the topic clearly.

Changing a slug safely

Try to choose the right slug before you publish, because changing it later can break links and lose traffic. If you must change one, set up a permanent redirect, often called a 301 redirect, from the old address to the new one. This sends visitors and search engines to the right page. Then update your internal links and your sitemap so they point to the new address. Do not change slugs just to make small improvements, because the risk is usually larger than the benefit.

Common mistakes

  • Using the default numbered or random addresses your website builder creates.
  • Stuffing several keywords into one long slug.
  • Changing slugs regularly without redirects.
  • Using the same slug pattern for different topics, which causes confusing addresses.
  • Including words that might become outdated, such as "new" or "latest".

A simple slug checklist

  1. Three to five words
  2. Lowercase letters and numbers only
  3. Hyphens between words
  4. Main topic included
  5. No filler words, dates or symbols

Conclusion

A good slug is short, readable and descriptive. It helps people trust your links and gives search engines a clear hint about your page. Choose it carefully before publishing, keep it stable afterwards, and use a tool to clean it up so you never have to worry about stray symbols or capital letters.
