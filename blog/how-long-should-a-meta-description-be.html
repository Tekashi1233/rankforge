How Long Should a Meta Description Be? (With Examples)

The meta description is the short summary that sits under your page title in search results. It is only a sentence or two, yet it often decides whether a person clicks your page or the one below it. A common question from beginners is how long it should be. This guide answers that, shows you how Google treats descriptions, and gives you a simple method for writing ones that work.

What is a meta description?

A meta description is an HTML tag placed in the head of a page. It tells search engines, and people reading search results, what the page is about. It looks like this in your code: a tag named description with your summary written inside it. Visitors never see it on the page itself. They only see it in search results, and sometimes in link previews on social platforms.

The ideal meta description length

A safe target is between 120 and 160 characters. Google does not set a strict character limit. Instead, it shortens snippets based on how much space they take up in pixels, which depends on the letters you use and on the device. Mobile results often show a little less than desktop results. In practice, descriptions longer than about 160 characters risk being cut off with an ellipsis, while very short ones waste a chance to persuade.

Because the end of a long description might be hidden, put the most important information first. If the first 100 characters make a clear promise, it does not matter much if the rest is trimmed. Does the meta description affect rankings?

Not directly. Google has said that descriptions are not a ranking factor. However, a better description can raise your click-through rate, meaning more people choose your result. More visitors who find what they wanted is good for your site overall. Think of the description as a small advertisement for your page rather than a ranking trick.

Why Google sometimes rewrites yours

You may notice that Google shows a different snippet than the one you wrote. This often happens when the page text matches the searcher's words better than your description does. You cannot fully control this, but you can reduce it by writing descriptions that clearly match what the page covers, and by making sure the page itself answers the question the search is asking.

How to write a meta description that gets clicks

  1. Match the search intent. If people want a how-to guide, say that you offer one. If they want a list, say so.
  2. Lead with the benefit. Tell people what they will learn or get, not what your company is.
  3. Use your main keyword naturally. Search engines highlight words that match the search, which catches the eye. Never force it in.
  4. Add a gentle call to action. Phrases like "learn how", "see examples" or "try the free tool" give a reason to click.
  5. Be honest. Promise only what the page delivers. Misleading descriptions cause people to leave quickly.
  6. Make each one unique. Every page should have its own description.

Good and bad examples

Weak: "Home page. Welcome to our website. We offer many services at great prices. Contact us today."

This tells the searcher nothing specific. It could describe any website.

Stronger: "Learn how long a meta description should be, with five examples and a free preview tool. Write snippets people actually click."

This names the topic, offers a clear benefit, mentions a tool, and ends with an outcome. It is about 130 characters, so it should display in full.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving it empty. Google will pick text from the page, which is often a poor summary.
  • Copying the same description everywhere. Duplicates make your pages look alike.
  • Stuffing keywords. A list of phrases reads badly and gets ignored.
  • Writing too much. A paragraph will simply be cut off.
  • Using only capital letters or symbols. It looks spammy and wastes space.

A quick checklist

  1. Between 120 and 160 characters
  2. Main benefit in the first half
  3. Main keyword used naturally
  4. A reason to click
  5. Unique to this page
  6. Accurate to the page content

Conclusion

A good meta description is short, specific and honest. Aim for around 150 characters, put your promise first, and write it as if you were persuading a real person to visit. You will not always control what Google shows, but writing clear descriptions gives your pages the best chance of earning the click.
