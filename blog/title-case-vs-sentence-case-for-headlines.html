Title Case vs Sentence Case: Which Is Better for Headlines?

Quick answer: Neither style is better for SEO, because search engines do not treat capitalization as a ranking signal. Pick one style and use it consistently across your titles and headings.

Headlines can be written in different capitalization styles, and the two most common are title case and sentence case. Neither is wrong, and neither is a ranking factor, but consistency makes your site look professional. This guide explains both styles and how to choose.

What is title case?

In title case, the main words in a headline start with a capital letter. Example: "How to Write a Strong Meta Description". Short, minor words such as articles, short prepositions and conjunctions usually stay lowercase unless they are the first or last word.

What is sentence case?

In sentence case, only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized, exactly as in a normal sentence. Example: "How to write a strong meta description". Words like names, places and brands, such as Google or London, keep their capitals.

Main rules for title case

  • Capitalize the first and last words.
  • Capitalize nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs and pronouns.
  • Keep articles (a, an, the), coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or) and short prepositions (in, on, at, of) lowercase.
  • Style guides such as AP, Chicago and APA differ on details, especially on which prepositions count as short, so choose one and stay with it.

Which is better for SEO?

Neither. Search engines do not treat capitalization as a ranking signal. What matters is clarity and click appeal. Some people find title case looks more formal and eye-catching in search results, while others find sentence case easier to read and friendlier. Test what suits your audience. How to choose

  • Title case suits traditional publications, formal brands and book-style titles.
  • Sentence case suits modern, conversational sites, apps and documentation, and is simpler to apply correctly.
  • Consistency matters more than the choice. Use the same style for all titles and headings across your site.

Common title-case mistakes

Most title-case errors come from capitalizing too much, not too little. When every word starts with a capital, the headline looks shouty and the important words stop standing out. The usual culprits are short words that should stay lowercase: "a", "an", "the", "and", "but", "or", "in", "on", "at" and "of". Compare "How To Write A Strong Meta Description" with the correct "How to Write a Strong Meta Description" — the second version is easier to scan because only the main words draw the eye.

Two other mistakes are easy to miss. First, forgetting to capitalize the last word: in title case the last word always takes a capital, even if it is a short preposition, as in "What to Look For". Second, lowercasing a proper noun or an acronym by accident after running text through a converter. "SEO", "Google" and "London" keep their capitals in both styles, so always read a converted headline once before you publish it.

A quick checklist before you publish a headline

Use this short check every time you write a title or heading, and most capitalization problems disappear:

  • Pick one style for the whole site. Decide title case or sentence case once, write it down in your style notes, and do not switch between pages.
  • Capitalize the first and last words. This rule holds in title case no matter how short those words are.
  • Check proper nouns and acronyms. Names, places, brands and abbreviations such as SEO, HTML and PDF keep their capitals in either style.
  • Read the headline out loud. If a capitalized word looks wrong or a lowercase word looks like a typo, it probably is.
  • Match the heading to the page. The headline, the page title and the slug should all describe the same topic, so a reader who clicks through is not surprised.
