Title Case vs Sentence Case: Which Is Better for Headlines?

Every headline you write forces a small decision: should the words start with capital letters, or only the first one? The two main styles are title case and sentence case. Neither is wrong, but mixing them carelessly makes a website look messy. This guide explains the difference, the basic rules, and how to choose one style and stay consistent.

What is title case?

In title case, most of the important words start with a capital letter. Small connecting words, such as articles, short prepositions and conjunctions, stay in lowercase unless they come first or last. For example: "How to Write a Great Headline for Your Blog". Title case is common in book titles, news headlines in some publications, and many blog post titles.

What is sentence case?

In sentence case, only the first word and any proper nouns start with a capital letter, just like a normal sentence. The same headline would read: "How to write a great headline for your blog". Many modern websites and apps prefer sentence case because it looks relaxed and easy to read.

The basic title case rules

Style guides differ in the details, but most agree on these points.

  • Capitalize the first and last word.
  • Capitalize nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs and pronouns.
  • Keep articles such as "a", "an" and "the" lowercase.
  • Keep short conjunctions such as "and", "but" and "or" lowercase.
  • Keep short prepositions such as "in", "on", "of" and "to" lowercase. Some guides capitalize prepositions of four or more letters.

Major style guides, such as AP and Chicago, disagree on a few cases, so pick one approach and apply it everywhere.

Which is better for SEO?

Search engines do not rank pages based on capitalization. A title in title case and the same title in sentence case are treated the same way. The choice affects how your result looks to people, not how it ranks. What matters is that your title is clear, specific and accurate, as we explain in our meta description guide and the on-page SEO checklist.

How to choose between them

  • Choose title case if you want a traditional, formal or editorial feel, or if your industry commonly uses it.
  • Choose sentence case if you want a friendly, modern and conversational tone, or if you write long headlines that are easier to read with fewer capitals.
  • Think about your audience. Look at the sites your readers already use and match what feels natural.

Whichever you pick, write it down as a rule for your site. That way your titles, headings, buttons and menus all follow the same pattern.

Where to keep it consistent

  1. Blog post titles and page titles
  2. Headings inside articles
  3. Menu items and buttons
  4. Social media headlines
  5. Email subject lines

You can use different styles for different jobs, for example title case for page titles and sentence case for subheadings, as long as you apply each one consistently.

Special cases to watch

  • Brand names. Keep the official spelling, such as iPhone or YouTube.
  • Acronyms. Words like SEO, URL and HTML are usually written in capitals.
  • Words after a colon. Many guides capitalize the first word after a colon in title case.
  • Hyphenated words. Styles differ, so check your chosen guide.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing titles in all capitals, which is hard to read and feels like shouting.
  • Switching styles between pages.
  • Capitalizing every single word, including small connecting words.
  • Trusting an automatic converter without a quick check.

Conclusion
