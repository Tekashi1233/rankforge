What is lorem ipsum?


Lorem ipsum is the standard placeholder text used by designers, developers, and typographers when the real content is not yet available. It is a scrambled version of a passage from Cicero’s “De finibus bonorum et malorum” (45 BCE). Because the text looks roughly like natural language but carries no meaning, it lets people judge layout, spacing, and typography without being distracted by readable sentences.


The words come from a real Latin text, but they have been chopped up and rearranged until the meaning is gone. The opening line, "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet", is a scrambled piece of "dolorem ipsum quia dolor sit amet", which roughly means "pain itself, because it is pain, one seeks it". The text has been used by printers since at least the 1500s, and it became the standard placeholder when desktop publishing spread in the 1980s. Its staying power is simple: it looks like real copy without saying anything, so the eye judges the design instead of reading the words.



When (and when not) to use placeholder text


Use lorem ipsum during the design and development stages: wireframes, mockups, theme demos, and client presentations. Replace it with real, useful content before the page goes live. Search engines and AI systems prefer original, helpful writing. Leaving “Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet…” on a published page is a common sign of an unfinished site and can hurt both user trust and SEO.

A worked example: testing a layout with the right amount of text


Suppose you are designing a blog card that will show the first two lines of each post. Generating one short paragraph and pasting it in tells you very little, because a real excerpt is 25 to 35 words, not 150. The useful test is to generate the amount of text the finished design will actually hold, then check three things:


    

  1. Does the text fit the space, or does it overflow and push other elements around?
    2. 

  2. Does the card still look balanced when the text is at its longest, not just its average length?
    3. 

  3. Can a reader scan the card quickly, or does the wall of grey text hide the headline?
    4. 



Run the same check with the shortest realistic text too. A layout that only works with exactly the right amount of words will break the first time a writer files a shorter post.


Placeholder text or real sample text?


Lorem ipsum is best when the design itself is the thing being judged: type sizes, line spacing, column widths. But when you show a design to a client or a colleague, real words work better, because people react to what the page says as well as how it looks. A headline about the actual product, a paragraph that describes a real feature, or a rough draft of the final copy all give better feedback than grey filler. A good rule is to use lorem ipsum early, while the layout is still moving around, and switch to realistic copy as soon as the design settles, even if the copy is only a rough draft.


Common mistakes with placeholder text


    

  • Publishing a page that still contains it. This is the classic one, and it happens when placeholder text sits in a footer, a sidebar or a help page that nobody re-checks before launch.
    • 

  • Testing with too little text. A design that fits three short paragraphs can fall apart when the real copy runs to ten.
    • 

  • Testing with too much text. The reverse is just as bad: filler that is longer than the real content hides the fact that the finished page will look empty.
    • 

  • Using it for content that search engines will read. Placeholder text carries no meaning, so a page full of it tells search engines nothing about the topic.
    • 

  • Letting it shape the writing. When writers see filler in place, they sometimes write to fill the space rather than to say what needs saying. Write the copy first, then fit the design around it.
    • 



Alternatives to lorem ipsum


If filler text feels wrong for the job, there are a few honest substitutes. A rough draft of the real copy is the best one, even if it is only a few sentences. Structured sample content also works: a real headline, a real subheading, a short list of real features. Some designers write plain-English sample copy about the product itself, which reads naturally and still leaves room for the final wording. What matters is not which filler you use, but that the words in the mockup are close in length and shape to the words that will replace them.


Frequently asked questions


What is lorem ipsum?
Placeholder text used to show how a layout looks before real content is ready.


Should I publish lorem ipsum?
No. Replace it with real content before your page goes live, since search engines prefer useful, original writing.


Why does the text start with the same phrase?
By tradition the first sentence is "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet". The rest is generated randomly each time.


Is lorem ipsum good for SEO testing?
No. Use real or realistic sample content when testing how search engines and AI tools interpret a page.



How many paragraphs should I generate for a mockup?
As many as the real content will have. For a blog card, one short paragraph is enough. For a full article layout, generate enough paragraphs to match the finished post, so the design is tested at real length.


Is lorem ipsum copyrighted?
No. It is derived from a Latin text written by Cicero in 45 BCE, long out of copyright. You can use it freely in mockups and designs.
