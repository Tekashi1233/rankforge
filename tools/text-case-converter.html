What is a text case converter?

A text case converter changes the capitalization of letters in a block of text without altering the words themselves. Common options include UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case (also called headline style), and Sentence case. These tools save time when you need to reformat titles, headings, product names, or bulk text for consistency.

Why consistent capitalization matters

Consistent case improves professionalism and readability. Search engines do not rank pages higher simply because of title case versus sentence case, but clear, consistent styling helps users scan pages and builds brand trust. Title Case is traditional for many headlines and book titles; Sentence case feels more modern and conversational and is increasingly common on the web and in UI copy.

How to use this Text Case Converter

Paste or type any text, then click one of the four buttons:

  • UPPERCASE — Every letter becomes capital.
  • lowercase — Every letter becomes small.
  • Title Case — Major words are capitalized; small connecting words (a, an, the, and, of, etc.) stay lowercase unless they start the phrase.
  • Sentence case — Only the first letter of each sentence is capitalized.

Line breaks and punctuation are preserved. Copy the result with one click. Everything runs locally in your browser.

When to use each style

  • Title Case — Traditional headlines, book titles, product names, navigation labels.
  • Sentence case — Modern web headings, UI text, blog post titles that aim for a conversational tone.
  • UPPERCASE — Emphasis, short labels, or stylistic design choices (use sparingly for readability).
  • lowercase — Stylistic choice, usernames, or cleaning up accidentally capitalized text.

Best practices

  • Pick one primary style for headings across your site and stick to it.
  • Be consistent within the same page or document.
  • Avoid all-caps for long headings — it reduces readability and can feel like shouting.
  • When converting Title Case, double-check small words and proper nouns that the automatic rules may miss.

Title case rules, in plain English

Title case has a few simple rules that cover most headlines:

  • Capitalize the first and last words, whatever they are.
  • Capitalize nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs and pronouns. In "How to Write a Strong Meta Description", the capitalized words are How, Write, Strong, Meta and Description.
  • Keep articles (a, an, the), short prepositions (in, on, at, of, to) and coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or) lowercase when they sit in the middle.

Style guides disagree on the details. AP, Chicago and APA all handle longer prepositions and small words slightly differently, so pick one guide and stay with it. This tool keeps a fixed list of small words lowercase: a, an, and, as, at, but, by, for, in, nor, of, on, or, the, to, up and via. Anything not on that list gets a capital in Title Case, so check the result if your guide says otherwise.

Common mistakes when changing case

  • Sentence case wipes out proper nouns. "I use Google daily" becomes "I use google daily". Check names, brands and places after converting and fix them by hand.
  • Sentence case wipes out acronyms. "SEO" becomes "Seo" and "USA" becomes "Usa". The tool cannot tell an acronym from an ordinary word, so acronyms need a manual fix.
  • Converting text that contains links or addresses. Case can matter in URLs, email addresses and file names. Convert only the display text, not the address itself.
  • Using UPPERCASE for whole paragraphs. A few words in capitals can add emphasis. A whole paragraph in capitals is harder to scan and reads like shouting, so save it for short labels.
  • Mixing styles on one page. A page with Title Case headings next to sentence case headings looks unfinished. Pick one style for headings and use it everywhere.

Where each style shows up in real work

Case conversion is one of those small jobs that comes up constantly. A spreadsheet export arrives in all capitals and needs cleaning before it goes into a report. Product titles copied from a supplier need Title Case before they go on a store page. Email subject lines, video titles and social media posts all read better when the case matches the rest of your content. Writers also use it to fix text copied from PDFs, which often arrives with odd capitalization, and to tidy up headings pasted from one document into another. Doing it by hand is slow and error prone, which is why a one-click converter earns its place.

A quick checklist before you publish a heading

  • Use the same case style for every heading on the site, not just on one page.
  • After converting, scan for proper nouns, brand names and acronyms that lost their capitals.
  • Check small words at the start and end of the heading. The first and last words should be capitalized in Title Case.
  • Read the heading aloud. If it sounds formal and polished, Title Case is doing its job. If it sounds plain and friendly, sentence case is.
  • Keep all-caps to a few words at most, such as a short label or a warning.

Frequently asked questions

What is title case?

A style where major words start with a capital letter, while small words such as "and", "of" and "the" stay lowercase unless they come first.

Is sentence case better for headings?

Both work. Pick one style and use it consistently across your site.

Does it keep my line breaks?

Yes. Only the letter case changes.

Does capitalization affect SEO?

Not directly. Search engines treat most case differences as the same words. Consistency and clarity for users matter more.

Does sentence case change proper nouns?

Yes. This tool lowercases the whole text first and then capitalizes only the start of each sentence, so Google becomes google and SEO becomes Seo. Check names, brands, places and acronyms after converting and fix them by hand.

Why did Title Case leave a word lowercase?

Title Case keeps small connecting words lowercase, including a, an, the, and, of and to, unless the word comes first. That matches most headline styles. If your style guide says otherwise, edit that word by hand after converting.
