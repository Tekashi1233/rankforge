What is a GEO Score?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the practice of making content easier for AI answer engines — Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and others — to find, understand and cite. A GEO Score is a simple 0–100 estimate of how well a piece of text is structured for those systems in 2027, when AI answers are expected to handle more than half of informational queries.

What this tool checks

  • Answer-first opening — Does the first 1–2 sentences give a clear, self-contained answer?
  • Statistics & numbers — Presence of specific figures, percentages or data points that engines like to quote.
  • Scannability — Short paragraphs, lists, and question-style language that help RAG systems extract passages.
  • Length balance — Enough substance to be useful without long walls of text.
  • Quotability signals — Clear statements that can stand alone when cited.

How to use the GEO Score Checker

1. Paste any section of content (a full post, a product description, an FAQ answer, or a knowledge-base article).
2. The score updates instantly as you type or paste.
3. Read the breakdown to see which factors are strong and which need work.
4. Improve the weak areas and re-check until the score is in a healthy range (aim for 70+).

Why this matters in 2027

By 2027, more than half of informational searches are expected to be answered directly by AI systems rather than a list of blue links. Content that is structured for extraction — clear answers up front, concrete data, short paragraphs, and quotable statements — is far more likely to appear in those answers. Traditional keyword density still helps discovery, but the final citation decision increasingly depends on how cleanly a passage can be lifted and attributed.

How the score is calculated

The score is built from six factors, adding up to 100:

  • Answer-first opening: 25 points. The first 180 characters give a clear, self-contained answer, or open with a complete statement of the point. A weak or missing opening earns 8 points.
  • Numbers and statistics: 20 points. Three or more specific numbers earns the full 20. One or two numbers earns 12. No numbers at all earns 3.
  • Lists and structure: 15 points. Two or more list items earns 15. One list item earns 8.
  • Short paragraphs: 15 points. Two or more paragraphs with an average under 90 words earns 15. Two or more longer paragraphs earns 7.
  • Length balance: 15 points. Between 150 and 1200 words earns the full 15. Over 80 words earns 8. Shorter text earns 3.
  • Question language: 10 points. At least one question mark in the text earns 10. None earns 4.

Knowing the weights tells you where the work is. The opening and the numbers are worth 45 points between them, so those two fixes move the score more than anything else.

What a high score does not mean

The score is a heuristic, a shortcut based on text patterns that answer engines tend to prefer. It is not a measure of quality, accuracy or authority. A page can score 90 and never be cited, because another site is more authoritative or more up to date. A page can score 60 and still be cited, because it is the best source available for that question. Crawlability, freshness and brand signals all sit outside what this tool can see. Use the score to make good content easier to extract and quote, not as a promise of a citation.

Common mistakes when optimizing for GEO

  • Opening with background instead of the answer. History and context belong further down the page. The first two sentences should answer the question.
  • Numbers with no context. "Sales grew" tells an answer engine nothing. "Sales grew 34 percent in 2025" is a statement it can quote.
  • One long wall of text. Long paragraphs are hard to lift cleanly. Short paragraphs and lists give answer engines clear passages to extract.
  • Adding a question mark only to gain points. A question readers genuinely ask works better than a question bolted on for the score. Write the question your reader is already thinking.
  • Pasting navigation and footer text with the article. Menus, sidebars and footers add words and paragraphs that distort the breakdown. Paste the main body text only.
  • Rewriting until the text reads like a checklist. Structure helps, but the page still has to read well for humans. Keep the sentences natural.

Frequently asked questions

Is a high GEO Score a guarantee of being cited?

No. The score is a heuristic based on observable text patterns. Authority, crawlability, freshness, and brand signals still matter. A high score simply means the text itself is structured in a way that answer engines prefer.

Does the tool send my content to a server?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Your text never leaves your device.

What score should I aim for?

70 or higher is a solid target for most informational content. Scores above 85 usually mean the text is already very well structured for AI extraction.

Can I check an entire page?

Yes. Paste the main body text. The tool works best on the content that would actually be cited (skip navigation, footers and boilerplate).

Why did my score drop after I added more text?

Length is scored in bands. Text between 150 and 1200 words earns the full length points, while very long text earns fewer, and longer text can also push the average paragraph length up. If the score drops, split the new text into short paragraphs and check the breakdown to see which factor changed.

Does the checker understand my topic?

No. It reads structure only: the opening, numbers, lists, paragraph length, word count and questions. It cannot judge whether the content is accurate, original or expert. A high score means the text is easy to extract and quote, not that it is right.
